## Methods for finding more work emails

### Official sources first
Check the company’s people and contact pages, named professional biographies and current conference biographies. Record the exact address, source URL, date and whether it is named or shared.

### Ask for a referral
If only a shared inbox is public, send a short, relevant request to be directed to the named person or the person responsible for BQS workflows. A shared address is not the individual’s address.

### LinkedIn or a warm introduction
Use the supplied name-and-company queries, confirm the current role, and ask for the preferred work email. IBIA, SIBCON and existing industry connections can provide introductions.

### Email-finder tools as leads
Hunter, Apollo or Snov.io may help identify work-email candidates. Their output is a lead, not official-source verification. Recheck identity, employer and source before adding it as a confirmed named contact.

### Verify delivery separately
A validator can check syntax, domain and some mailbox signals, but catch-all domains and blocked checks make results uncertain. No messages were sent and no mailbox deliverability tests were performed for this list.

### Gmail versus email
These are publicly published professional emails. A business domain works for outreach; a private Gmail account is unnecessary. No private Gmail accounts or guessed addresses are included.
